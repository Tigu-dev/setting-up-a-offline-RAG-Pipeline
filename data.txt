# Introduction to RAG (Retrieval Augmented Generation)

Retrieval-Augmented Generation, commonly called RAG, is an AI technique where a language model retrieves information from an external data source before generating an answer. This helps the model stay factual and grounded in your documents.

## Why RAG is useful
RAG is useful because:
- It avoids hallucinations by relying on actual documents.
- It allows updating knowledge without retraining the model.
- It enables domain-specific chatbots, such as legal or medical assistants.

## How a RAG pipeline works
A basic RAG pipeline contains three major steps:

1. **Indexing**  
   Your documents are split into chunks and converted into vector embeddings.

2. **Retrieval**  
   When you ask a question, the system retrieves the most similar chunks.

3. **Generation**  
   The LLM answers using ONLY the retrieved context.

## When RAG fails
A RAG system will fail or return "Not found in your documents" when:
- The question is unrelated to stored documents.
- The document chunks do not contain that information.
- The chunk size is too small or too large.

## Example facts
- The inventor of RAG is Meta AI, introduced in 2020.
- RAG is widely used in chatbots, search engines, and enterprise AI systems.
- RAG works best when documents are clean and well-split.
- Embedding models convert text into numerical vectors.

## Fun fact
RAG is not a single algorithm—it is a design pattern combining retrieval + generation.
